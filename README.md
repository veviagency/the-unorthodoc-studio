# The UnOrthoDoc Vision

Create a premium, high-fidelity, fully responsive website prototype for The UnOrthoDoc, Dr. Patrice Smith’s personal brand platform.

This must feel like a real, polished, launch-ready website that could be shared directly with Dr. Patrice as a design concept. It should not look like a generic template, a dentist website, or a simple creator landing page.

Brand context

Dr. Patrice Smith is a practicing orthodontist, writer, wife, mother of three, entrepreneur, and founder of The UnOrthoDoc.

The UnOrthoDoc is her personal media, education, product, and partnership platform. Its themes include:

Personal development

Professional development

Financial growth

Entrepreneurship and side hustles

Motherhood

Oral care and smile health

Building a meaningful life outside a single professional identity

The existing Infinity Orthodontics website is her clinical practice website. Do not merge the two brands.

Infinity Orthodontics should appear only as a subtle external destination for visitors looking for orthodontic treatment. The UnOrthoDoc should remain a personal brand and thought-leadership platform, not a patient booking website.

Use a discreet line such as:

“Looking for orthodontic care in Washington, DC? Visit Infinity Orthodontics.”

Place this in the footer and optionally on the About page. Do not place clinical booking, insurance, treatment plans, patient forms, or medical content in the main navigation.

Core goal

Create a clearer, more premium, more structured experience where a first-time visitor immediately understands where they belong and where to go next.

A visitor should quickly be able to identify one of these paths:

I want thoughtful writing and weekly reflections.

I want to build something outside my career.

I want deeper guidance and a membership community.

I want practical resources, digital products, or planners.

I represent a brand and want to work with Dr. Patrice.

The design should preserve the richness of her brand without making the visitor choose between too many unrelated links at once.

Homepage structure

Build the homepage in this order.

1. Minimal announcement area

A restrained, optional line above the navigation for something like:

“The Climb arrives every Sunday.”

Do not use loud banners, countdown timers, or promotional popups.

2. Editorial hero

Create a refined split-screen hero on desktop and a strong stacked layout on mobile.

Use a warm, confident portrait area for Dr. Patrice. If importing public imagery from the existing websites is not technically possible, use elegant image placeholders that are clearly easy to replace later.

Suggested copy:

Eyebrow:
The UnOrthoDoc

Headline:
Build a life that fits.

Supporting copy:
Thoughtful reflections, practical resources, and honest ideas for professionals building meaningful work, stronger finances, and a full life beyond one title.

Primary CTA:
Join The Climb

Secondary CTA:
Explore the Journal

3. Find your way section

Directly below the hero, add a premium “Find your way” section with five visually distinct route cards.

Read and Reflect
The Journal, Sunday letters, and thoughtful essays on work and life.

Build Something of Your Own
The Side Hustle Blueprint and resources for professionals creating more options.

Go Deeper
The Climb+ for deeper reflections, private audio, guided prompts, and live conversations.

Explore Practical Resources
E-books, planners, guides, and useful tools.

Partner With Dr. Patrice
Thoughtful collaborations for brands serving modern professionals.

Each card must lead to a real route in the prototype.

4. Brand pillars

Group the existing content categories into three easy-to-understand editorial pillars instead of showing six equally competing categories.

Live Well
Personal growth, motherhood, identity, and meaningful living.

Build Well
Career, entrepreneurship, financial growth, and side hustles.

Care Well
Oral care, smile health, and trustworthy guidance from an orthodontist.

Use elegant editorial cards with images or image placeholders, subtle hover states, and clear links.

5. Featured writing

Create a curated editorial area with one featured article and three secondary article cards.

Use existing article titles only if they can be imported accurately. Otherwise use neutral placeholders such as “Featured reflection” and “Latest from the journal.” Do not invent testimonials, social proof, article metrics, or quotes.

6. The Climb newsletter section

Make this a beautiful, calm conversion section rather than a generic email form.

Use copy such as:

“The Climb is a Sunday letter for people building a meaningful life with ambition and intention.”

Include first name and email fields, a clear privacy note, and a polished confirmation state.

Do not show an email popup on initial page load. If a future email capture modal is included, it must appear only after meaningful scroll or exit intent, and never compete with a cookie consent message.

7. The Climb+ feature

Create a distinct paid-membership preview section that feels intimate and premium.

Include:

Deeper weekly reflections

Private audio reflections

Guided prompts

Growing resource library

Occasional live conversations and workshops

Use a sophisticated membership card and a “Explore The Climb+” CTA.

8. Featured learning and products

Use a polished editorial commerce section with:

The Side Hustle Blueprint

Digital planners and e-books

Optional apparel preview

The Side Hustle Blueprint should feel like a serious course for busy professionals, not a flashy internet-marketing product.

9. Collaboration section

Create a premium partnership section for brands.

Communicate that Dr. Patrice selectively partners with organizations aligned with modern professionals, healthcare and practice technology, productivity, financial platforms, wellness, and oral care.

Use a CTA such as:

“Explore collaboration opportunities”

Include a clean media-kit request form design.

Payments and integrations

Do not build live checkout, payment processing, memberships, login, fulfillment, CRM, or email-platform integrations yet.

Instead:

Build all purchase and membership flows as polished, integration-ready UI.

Make pricing, CTA URLs, product data, and membership plans easy to edit later.

Use editable placeholder actions such as PAYMENT_URL, MEMBERSHIP_CHECKOUT_URL, and EMAIL_PLATFORM_FORM_URL.

Do not collect card details.

Use a tasteful demo confirmation state for checkout buttons.

The goal is a shareable premium concept with clear future integration paths.

Visual direction

Use a modern editorial visual system.

The site should feel:

Premium

Calm

Intelligent

Human

Warm

Feminine without becoming overly soft or cliché

Professional without looking corporate

Contemporary without looking like a startup dashboard

Suggested palette:

Warm ivory or soft cream background

Deep ink navy or charcoal for text

Muted terracotta, dusty rose, or warm clay as an accent

Light stone or pale blue-gray for surfaces

No bright gradients, neon colors, or glassmorphism

Typography:

Elegant serif display type for major headlines

Clean modern sans-serif for body text and navigation

Large editorial headlines

Comfortable reading width

Body text must remain highly readable on mobile

Use generous whitespace, subtle texture, refined borders, understated motion, and excellent image framing.

Avoid:

Generic lifestyle stock photos

Fake client logos

Fake testimonials

Fake subscriber counts

Loud badges

Excessive animations

Carousels that require horizontal scrolling

Overcrowded cards

Medical imagery that makes this look like a clinic website

Mobile requirements

Mobile quality is critical.

Design and test specifically for a 390px-wide phone screen.

Requirements:

No horizontal scrolling

Minimum 16px readable body text

Minimum 44px tap targets

Clear hierarchy before the first scroll

Stacked hero layout with portrait, headline, copy, and CTA

Mobile menu with grouped navigation

Sticky contextual CTA at the bottom of key conversion pages

Product, article, and path cards must stack cleanly

Forms must be easy to complete with one hand

No oversized desktop-style whitespace or huge images that delay content

Desktop requirements

Also optimize for a 1440px desktop view.

Use a centered editorial layout with a maximum content width around 1200px to 1280px.

Desktop should include:

Elegant split hero

Clear visual rhythm

Three-column content grids where appropriate

Refined hover states

Strong whitespace

No excessive empty space

No dense navigation or link clutter

Technical and accessibility requirements

Build with reusable components and structured content data.

Use semantic headings and accessible contrast.

Use descriptive button labels.

Include visible focus states.

Keep all form states clear.

Make image placeholders easy to replace.

Keep copy and product data editable in one central location.

Use realistic placeholder images only where necessary.

Do not fabricate facts, credentials, reviews, campaign results, partnerships, or patient outcomes.

The final result should look like a thoughtful, premium personal brand ecosystem for Dr. Patrice Smith: one that clearly separates reading, learning, shopping, and collaboration while preserving her identity as a practicing orthodontist, writer, mother, and entrepreneur.
